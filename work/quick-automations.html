My role

Creating a foundation

I led the end-to-end design for Quick Automations, a real-time automation framework built specifically for conversational support workflows inside Freshdesk Omni.

From defining the information architecture to turning highly technical automation logic into approachable templates, I worked closely with Product, Engineering, CX and GTM teams to shape a scalable experience that could support omni-channel workflows without overwhelming admins.

The project became a foundational step toward Freshdesk's long-term workflow modernization vision.

Problem statement

Built for email. Strained by chat.

Freshdesk's legacy automation framework was designed for email-first support. It relied heavily on Hourly Triggers, which evaluate tickets periodically instead of instantly. That worked for email, but it became a major operational limitation on conversational channels where customers expect replies in seconds. Teams couldn't reliably run:

  • Instant acknowledgements
  • Delay-based customer reassurance
  • Smart fallback routing
  • Real-time follow-ups
  • Faster SLA-driven workflows
  • Multi-channel orchestration

Meanwhile Zendesk and Intercom already supported second-level conversational automation, making real-time execution a market expectation rather than a premium capability.

How might we

Three questions, one north star.

  1. Help support teams automate real-time conversations without complex setup?
  2. Execute automation rules within seconds instead of hours?
  3. Simplify conversational automation for SMB admins while supporting omni-channel scale?
Research

Why the existing system kept falling short.

Before designing Quick Automations, I needed to understand why Freshdesk's existing framework struggled with conversational workflows. The findings validated the need for a dedicated real-time layer and showed how dramatically customer expectations had shifted.

Email-first architecture

Tickets were evaluated periodically via Hourly Triggers. Reliable for email, but unfit for channels that demand instant responses.

Expectations had changed

Research with 18 CX operations leads showed 94% of teams expected automated acknowledgements within 30–90 seconds on chat. Delays directly hurt CSAT and SLA.

Delay & cancel became critical

Automations had to stop if an agent replied first. Existing systems lacked this, creating duplicate or irrelevant replies.

Builders were too complex

Template setup averaged 2.3 minutes vs 8.7 for builders, and errors dropped from 2.4 to 0.2 per setup.

Speed beat flexibility

Teams consistently prioritized faster setup, execution and acknowledgement over highly customizable but complex workflow systems.

Key takeaways

  • Real-time channels require second-level automation responsiveness
  • Traditional automation models fail for conversational workflows
  • Delay-and-cancel execution is critical for chat-based support
  • Users preferred guided templates over complex workflow builders
  • Conversational automation needed a fundamentally different execution architecture
Deep dive into user behaviour

Admins didn't want to build workflows.

Through stakeholder interviews, workflow analysis and operational mapping, one insight kept surfacing: conversational support teams behaved very differently from email-first teams. They needed instant fallback workflows, automated reassurance, fast setup and a single place to manage multi-channel automations.

“Admins didn't want to build workflows. They wanted the system to intelligently handle operational gaps automatically.”

Email automations were treated as long-term process configuration. Conversational automations were expected to be set up and deployed within minutes, often during live support shifts. That pushed us to prioritize guided templates over fully customizable builders.

Research objectives

  1. Understand operational gaps in conversational support workflows
  2. Identify limitations in the existing automation system
  3. Understand execution-speed expectations for real-time channels
  4. Reduce setup complexity for SMB admins
  5. Explore scalable patterns for future workflow unification

Key findings

  1. Customers expected automation to run within seconds, not minutes
  2. The most urgent use cases were delayed responses and fallback routing
  3. Existing automation felt too technical for SMB admins
  4. Template-based setup significantly reduced onboarding friction
  5. Real-time automation created immediate operational value
  6. Multi-channel support made automation significantly more relevant
The 6 templates

Six high-frequency gaps. Six templates.

To simplify setup for SMB and mid-market admins, I designed Quick Automations around guided templates instead of asking users to configure workflows from scratch. Each template targets a high-frequency operational gap observed during research.

Keep customers informed of delays

Sends reassurance messages when agents take longer than expected. Delay-and-cancel logic stops the message if an agent replies first.

Reassign to available agent

Unassigns and reroutes conversations when the assigned agent doesn't respond in time, so chats never get stuck.

Reroute to fallback group

Moves conversations to backup groups during peak load, helping teams keep SLA commitments through traffic spikes.

Follow up with inactive customers

Nudges customers who stop responding mid-conversation, reviving stalled chats without manual effort.

Reduce load for agents

Changes ticket status when customers stay inactive, clearing queues and freeing agents for fresh conversations.

Keep track of resolution delays

Tags tickets that stay unresolved past a threshold, like SLA risk or waiting_in_queue, so teams can step in before resolution times slip.

Competitor analysis

A strategic bridge to real-time.

I analyzed conversational automation across Zendesk, Zoho, Salesforce and Intercom. Each offered powerful systems, but all relied on block-based builders that take extensive setup. Freshdesk had no modern builder yet, and Hourly Triggers couldn't meet 30-second SLAs. Quick Automations became the bridge from legacy email-first automation to real-time orchestration.

Builders favoured flexibility over speed

Configuring even simple conversational workflows took multiple steps and technical understanding.

Conversational support became the norm

WhatsApp, social DMs and web chat were growing much faster than email, raising the need for real-time tooling.

SMB teams needed simpler automation

Existing systems were too complex for smaller teams, a clear opening for guided templates.

Legacy automation was built for async

Periodic ticket evaluation was poorly suited to support where execution speed directly shapes CX.

Our edge

  • Instant rule execution in as little as 30 seconds
  • Template-first conversational automation
  • Real-time delay-and-cancel workflows
  • Multi-channel support orchestration
  • Simplified setup for SMB admins

What's next

A product is never finished.

Quick Automations was the first step in a broader workflow modernization effort. The long-term vision unifies legacy Automations and Quick Automations under a single Workflows platform that handles both asynchronous and real-time support automation.

The next phase focuses on richer automation actions, smarter execution, expanded orchestration and eventually a modern block-based builder, while keeping the simplicity and speed that made Quick Automations work for conversational support.
