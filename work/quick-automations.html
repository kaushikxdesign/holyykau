Quick Automations

An instant automation framework for conversational support, running workflows in as little as 30 seconds across WhatsApp, Web Chat, Facebook DM and Instagram DM.

TeamPM, Eng, CX, GTM

01 — Overview

Creating a foundation

I led the end-to-end design for Quick Automations, a real-time automation framework built for conversational support workflows inside Freshdesk Omni.

From defining the information architecture to turning highly technical automation logic into approachable templates, I worked closely with Product, Engineering, CX and GTM teams on an experience that could support omni-channel workflows without overwhelming admins.

The project became a foundational step toward Freshdesk's long-term workflow modernization vision.

02 — Problem

Built for email. Strained by chat.

Freshdesk's legacy automation framework was designed for email-first support. It relied on Hourly Triggers, which evaluate tickets periodically instead of instantly. That worked for email, but it was a major limitation on conversational channels where customers expect replies in seconds.

  • Instant acknowledgements
  • Delay-based customer reassurance
  • Faster SLA-driven workflows
  • Multi-channel orchestration

Meanwhile Zendesk and Intercom already supported second-level conversational automation. Real-time execution had become something customers expected, not a premium extra.

03 — How might we

Three questions, one north star.

  1. Help support teams automate real-time conversations without complex setup?
  2. Execute automation rules within seconds instead of hours?
  3. Simplify conversational automation for SMB admins while supporting omni-channel scale?
04 — Research

Why the existing system kept falling short.

Research with 18 CX operations leads showed that 94% of teams expected automated acknowledgements within 30–90 seconds on chat channels.

Email-first architecture

Tickets were evaluated periodically. That was reliable for email but unfit for channels that need instant responses.

Expectations had changed

Delays directly hurt CSAT and SLA on chat-based channels.

Delay & cancel became critical

Automations had to stop if an agent replied first. Without that, customers got duplicate or irrelevant replies.

Builders were too complex

Templates averaged 2.3 minutes to set up vs 8.7 for builders. Errors dropped from 2.4 to 0.2 per setup.

05 — Behaviour

Admins didn't want to build workflows.

“Admins didn't want to build workflows. They wanted the system to intelligently handle operational gaps automatically.”

Email automations were treated as long-term process configuration. Conversational automations were expected to be set up and deployed within minutes, often during live support shifts. That pushed us to prioritize guided templates over fully customizable builders.

06 — Solution

Six high-frequency gaps. Six templates.

Keep customers informed of delays

Sends reassurance messages that auto-cancel if an agent replies first.

Reassign to available agent

Reroutes chats when the assigned agent doesn't respond in time.

Reroute to fallback group

Moves conversations to backup groups during peak load.

Follow up with inactive customers

Nudges customers who stop responding mid-conversation.

Reduce load

Changes ticket status when customers stay inactive, which clears the queue.

Track resolution delays

Tags tickets like SLA risk once they pass a wait threshold.

07 — Competitors

A strategic bridge to real-time.

Zendesk, Zoho, Salesforce and Intercom all relied on block-based builders that take significant setup. Freshdesk had no modern builder, and Hourly Triggers couldn't meet 30-second SLAs. Quick Automations became the bridge from legacy email-first automation to real-time orchestration.
